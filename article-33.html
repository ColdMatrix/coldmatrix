PEB Construction in Pakistan: Step-by-Step Process & Timeline (2026)

WA
Waseem Akhtar
Founder & CEO, Cold Matrix Advanced Solutions
✍️ Reviewed & Updated:

Pre-Engineered Buildings (PEB) have become the preferred choice for warehouses, factories, and cold storage facilities in Pakistan due to their speed, cost-effectiveness, and flexibility. Unlike conventional construction, PEB uses factory-fabricated steel components that are bolted together on site. This guide walks you through the entire PEB construction process, from initial planning to final handover.

1. Design & Engineering

The first step is the structural design. A licensed engineer prepares drawings based on:

  • Building dimensions (length, width, eave height)
  • Bay spacing (typically 6–8 meters)
  • Load requirements (crane, snow, wind, seismic)
  • Local building codes and soil conditions

The design also determines the steel tonnage, which is the main cost driver. In Pakistan, typical steel weight for a warehouse without crane is 10–12 kg/m² of floor area. With a crane, it can be 20–35 kg/m².

2. Fabrication

Once the design is approved, components are fabricated in a factory. This includes:

  • Main frames (columns and rafters)
  • Purlins and girts
  • Bracing and connection plates
  • Roof and wall sheeting (if included)

Fabrication takes 2–4 weeks depending on the size of the project. Quality control includes welding inspections and dimensional checks.

3. Site Preparation & Foundation

While fabrication is ongoing, the site is prepared:

  • Soil testing and compaction
  • Excavation for foundations
  • Casting of concrete footings and pedestals

Anchor bolts are embedded in the concrete to connect the steel columns. This step takes 1–2 weeks and is critical for alignment.

4. Steel Erection

Erection is the fastest part of PEB construction. A typical crew can erect a 10,000 ft² warehouse in 1–2 weeks.

The sequence usually is:

  1. Erect main frames and columns
  2. Install roof rafters and purlins
  3. Add bracing for stability
  4. Install wall girts

Cranes are used to lift the heavy components. Safety is paramount during this phase.

5. Sheeting & Cladding

After the steel skeleton is complete, the roof and wall panels are installed. In Pakistan, options include:

  • Single-skin metal sheets — economical for open warehouses.
  • Sandwich panels (PU/PIR) — required for insulated buildings (cold storage, food processing).

Sheeting takes 1–2 weeks. Proper sealing and fasteners are essential to prevent leaks.

6. Finishing & Accessories

Final steps include:

  • Doors, windows, and ventilation
  • Gutters and downspouts
  • Insulation (if not using sandwich panels)
  • Electrical and plumbing rough-ins

This phase adds 1–2 weeks.

7. Total Timeline & Cost

Design & engineering1–2 weeks
Fabrication2–4 weeks

Cost in Pakistan (2026):
Standard warehouse without insulation: Rs. 2,500–4,000 per ft² (excluding foundation).
Insulated (sandwich panel) warehouse: Rs. 4,000–6,500 per ft².

8. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not specifying crane loads upfront — this increases steel weight by 30% or more if added later.
  • Choosing wrong bay spacing — too wide increases steel cost; too narrow increases number of frames.
  • Ignoring soil conditions — poor soil requires deeper foundations.
  • Not planning for future expansion — PEBs can be expanded easily if designed correctly.

FAQ

Q: Is PEB cheaper than conventional construction in Pakistan?

A: Yes, for industrial buildings PEB is typically 10–20% cheaper due to less steel and faster construction.

Q: Can PEB be used for cold storage?

A: Absolutely. Combine PEB steel structure with insulated sandwich panels for a complete cold room.
