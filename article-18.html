Profit Margin vs Markup — The Difference That Costs Businesses Money

Published by ColdMatrix • 5 min read

This is one of the most common — and most costly — pricing mistakes small business owners make. Confusing these two numbers can mean pricing your products lower than you think.

The Core Difference

Markup is profit calculated as a percentage of your cost. Margin is profit calculated as a percentage of your selling price. Same profit, different base — and the percentages are never equal (margin is always the smaller number for any given profit).

The Formulas

Markup % = (Selling Price − Cost) �÷ Cost × 100
Margin % = (Selling Price − Cost) ÷ Selling Price × 100

A Worked Example

Say your cost is Rs. 100 and you sell for Rs. 150. Your markup is (150−100)÷100×100 = 50%. But your margin is (150−100)÷150×100 = 33.3%. Same Rs. 50 profit — very different percentages.

Why This Mistake Costs Money

If a business owner wants a 50% profit margin but mistakenly applies a 50% markup instead, they'll actually only achieve a 33.3% margin — undershooting their real profitability target without realizing it, potentially by a significant amount across thousands of transactions.

Which One Should You Use?

  • Use margin when analyzing overall business profitability and comparing against revenue
  • Use markup when setting individual product prices from your cost base
  • Always know which one you're calculating — many spreadsheet and pricing mistakes come from mixing the two
