What Is a Cold Storage Room?

A cold storage room is a temperature-controlled space used to preserve perishable goods such as food, pharmaceuticals, and industrial materials. It is built with insulated panels, a cooling system, and a vapour barrier to maintain a stable low temperature and protect products from spoilage.

Cold storage rooms are commonly used in:

  • Food processing and distribution
  • Restaurants and supermarkets
  • Pharmaceutical storage
  • Agricultural cold chains
  • Industrial test chambers

The performance of a cold storage room depends on correct insulation, accurate cooling load calculation, and proper installation of panels and doors.

How the Cold Storage Calculator Works

This calculator estimates the cooling load and helps you understand the key parameters for designing a cold room. It considers:

  • Room dimensions (length, width, height)
  • Internal and external temperatures
  • Insulation type and thickness
  • Product type and quantity
  • Door openings and daily usage

While it gives a reliable estimate, final cooling system selection should always be confirmed by a qualified HVAC or refrigeration engineer.

Key Inputs Explained

  • Room dimensions: Determines the total volume and surface area to be cooled.
  • External temperature: The hotter the outside air, the higher the cooling load.
  • Internal temperature: Depends on product storage requirements (e.g., -18°C for frozen, +2°C for fresh).
  • Insulation type: PU/PIR panels provide better thermal resistance than EPS.
  • Insulation thickness: Thicker insulation reduces heat gain and energy consumption.
  • Product type: Different products release different amounts of heat.
  • Daily door openings: Frequent openings increase warm air infiltration.

Real-World Example

Imagine a cold room with internal dimensions of 6 m × 4 m × 3 m. The outside temperature is 35°C, and the desired internal temperature is -18°C. The room uses 100 mm PU/PIR insulated panels.

  • Volume: 6 × 4 × 3 = 72 m³
  • Surface area: Approximately 108 m²
  • Temperature difference: 35°C - (-18°C) = 53°C
  • Estimated cooling load: High, due to large temperature difference and frozen storage

Using the calculator, the user can adjust insulation thickness and see how it reduces the estimated load. This helps in selecting the right panels and cooling unit.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Underestimating heat infiltration — Every door opening brings warm air inside. Always add a safety margin.
  • Wrong insulation thickness — Saving money on panels leads to high energy bills later.
  • Ignoring floor insulation — Cold rooms need floor insulation to prevent condensation and structural damage.
  • Poor vapour barrier installation — Moisture entering the panels reduces insulation performance and causes ice build-up.
  • Not considering product load — Products entering at higher temperature add extra load that must be removed quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: What is the best insulation for a cold storage room?
A: PU (Polyurethane) or PIR (Polyisocyanurate) panels are recommended because they offer the best thermal performance per mm of thickness. EPS panels can be used for less demanding applications.

Q: How thick should my panels be?
A: For frozen storage (-18°C), 100 mm to 150 mm PU/PIR is typical. For fresh products (+2°C), 60 mm to 80 mm is often sufficient, depending on climate.

Q: Can I use this calculator for a freezer room?
A: Yes, simply set the internal temperature to your desired freezer temperature and use appropriate insulation values.

Q: Is this calculator accurate enough for project execution?
A: It gives a reliable estimate for planning and budgeting. For final equipment selection, always consult a licensed HVAC engineer.
