How EMI Is Calculated — The Real Formula Behind Your Loan Repayment

Published by ColdMatrix • 6 min read

Every loan offer comes with an EMI number, but very few people understand how that number is actually produced. Here's the real math behind it, explained simply.

What EMI Actually Means

EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) is a fixed payment you make every month that covers both interest and principal. In the early months, most of your payment goes toward interest; in the later months, more goes toward principal. This is called amortization, and it's why paying off a loan early can save significant interest.

The Formula

Banks use this standard formula: EMI = [P × R × (1+R)^N] / [(1+R)^N − 1], where P is the principal loan amount, R is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and N is the loan tenure in months.

A Worked Example

Say you borrow Rs. 1,000,000 at 12% annual interest for 5 years (60 months). Your monthly rate R = 12/12/100 = 0.01. Plugging into the formula gives an EMI of approximately Rs. 22,244 per month — and over the full tenure, you'll pay roughly Rs. 334,640 in total interest.

Why Tenure Matters More Than People Realize

  • Doubling your tenure doesn't halve your EMI — it usually reduces it by only 30-40%, because you're paying interest for twice as long
  • Shorter tenure means higher EMI but dramatically less total interest paid
  • Making even one extra payment per year can shave years off a long-term loan

What to Watch For

Always check whether your quoted rate is flat rate or reducing balance rate — these produce very different actual costs even at the same advertised percentage. Reducing balance (used in the formula above) is the standard and fairer method, and what most banks use for personal, auto, and home loans.
