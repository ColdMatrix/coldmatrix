🌿 Complete Health Chart — Beyond BMI

BMI is just one number. Here's the fuller picture of what supports good health day to day.

Nutrition
Favor whole foods — fruits, vegetables, whole grains, lean protein. Limit ultra-processed foods and added sugar. Eat slowly and notice fullness.
Hydration
Sip water steadily through the day rather than all at once. Limit sugary drinks. Increase intake in heat or during exercise.
Movement & Workout
Mix cardio with strength training weekly. Short daily walks add up. Let rest days be part of the plan, not a setback.
Sleep & Lifestyle
Aim for 7–9 hours of quality sleep. Manage stress with hobbies, breathing exercises, or time outdoors. Keep routine check-ups.

📖 Understanding Your Results

What exactly is BMI?
Body Mass Index estimates body fat using your weight relative to your height. It's a fast, widely used screening tool — not a diagnosis — and the same formula is used by doctors worldwide as a starting point for a health conversation.
What are BMR and TDEE?
BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is roughly how much energy your body uses at complete rest just to keep your organs running. TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) adds your activity level on top of that, giving a general estimate of daily calorie needs. Both are educational estimates, not prescriptions.
What are BMI's limitations?
BMI doesn't distinguish muscle from fat, doesn't account for bone density, age, or where fat is stored, and can read differently for athletes, older adults, and pregnant women. It's one data point among many — pairing it with a waist measurement or a professional check-up gives a fuller picture.
Should I act on this alone?
Think of these numbers as a helpful starting point for a conversation with a doctor, dietitian, or trainer — not a final verdict on your health. Everybody's body is different.
