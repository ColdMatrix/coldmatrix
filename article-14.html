Understanding BMI — What Your Number Actually Means

Published by ColdMatrix • 5 min read

BMI (Body Mass Index) is one of the most widely used — and widely misunderstood — health metrics. Here's what it actually tells you, and where it falls short.

How BMI Is Calculated

Formula: BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ [Height (m)]². For example, someone weighing 70kg at 1.75m height has a BMI of 70 ÷ (1.75×1.75) = 22.9.

The Standard Categories (WHO)

BMI RangeCategory
Below 18.5Underweight
18.5 – 24.9Normal weight
25.0 – 29.9Overweight
30.0 and aboveObese

What BMI Gets Right

As a population-level screening tool, BMI correlates reasonably well with health risk across large groups, and it's simple, free, and requires no special equipment — which is why doctors and researchers still use it widely.

Where BMI Falls Short

  • It doesn't distinguish between muscle and fat — athletes and bodybuilders often show as "overweight" despite low body fat
  • It doesn't account for where fat is distributed, which matters more for health risk than total body fat
  • It uses the same formula regardless of age, sex, or ethnicity, even though healthy ranges genuinely differ across these groups

The Honest Takeaway

BMI is a useful starting point for a general conversation about health, not a diagnosis. If your number concerns you, or you have questions about muscle mass, body composition, or overall health, talk to a doctor rather than relying on BMI alone.
