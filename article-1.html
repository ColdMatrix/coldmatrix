How Sandwich Panels Reduce Indoor Temperature by 6-8°C

Published by ColdMatrix • 4 min read

In Pakistan and the subcontinent, summer temperatures routinely exceed 45°C. For warehouses, factories, cold stores, and even residential buildings, this extreme heat means high energy bills, uncomfortable working conditions, and reduced equipment lifespan.

But there's a proven solution: PUF/PIR sandwich panels — and they can reduce indoor temperatures by 6-8°C without any active cooling.

How Does It Work?

Sandwich panels consist of two metal skins (usually PPGI steel) bonded to a core of rigid polyurethane foam (PUF) or polyisocyanurate (PIR). This foam core has an incredibly low thermal conductivity — typically 0.022 W/m·K for PUF.

When sunlight hits a conventional roof or wall, heat transfers through the material into the building. With a sandwich panel, the insulating core acts as a thermal barrier, dramatically slowing down heat transfer.

Real-World Temperature Difference

From our installations across Karachi, Lahore, and Multan, we've consistently measured:

  • Conventional brick/concrete building: Indoor reaches 40-42°C on a 45°C day
  • With 50mm PUF sandwich panel roof: Indoor stays at 33-35°C
  • With 75mm panels: Indoor drops further to 31-33°C

That's a 6-8°C reduction — purely from insulation, with zero energy cost.

Benefits Beyond Cooling

  • Lower AC costs: Smaller units needed, lower electricity bills
  • Year-round comfort: Keeps warmth in during colder months
  • Sound insulation: Reduces outside noise
  • Fire resistance: PIR panels meet international fire safety standards
  • Fast installation: Prefabricated panels install in days, not weeks

The Bottom Line

Whether you're building a cold room at -25°C or trying to survive a 45°C Karachi summer, sandwich panels are one of the most cost-effective investments you can make.
