5 Critical Mistakes to Avoid When Building a Cold Storage Room

By Waseem Akhtar
Founder & CEO, Cold Matrix Advanced Solutions
Published: November 2026

Building a cold storage facility is a major investment. Over the years, we have been called in to repair or overhaul many cold rooms that were built by inexperienced contractors. These mistakes cost business owners millions in spoiled products and sky-high electricity bills.

Here are the 5 most critical mistakes to avoid when designing and installing a cold room.

1. Skipping the Vapor Barrier (Plastic Sheet)

This is the #1 cause of cold room failure. When you lay XPS floor insulation and pour wet concrete over it, the moisture from the concrete will seep into the insulation unless there is a heavy-duty plastic vapor barrier between them. Once the XPS gets wet, it loses its insulation value entirely, and the floor will eventually freeze and crack.

2. Using the Wrong Insulation for the Floor

You cannot use the same panels for the walls and the floor. The floor requires high-compressive strength insulation to bear the weight of forklifts and heavy racks. We always specify high-density XPS (Extruded Polystyrene) for the floor, never standard PU boards.

3. Undersizing the Refrigeration System

Many contractors try to save money by buying a smaller condensing unit. But if the system cannot pull down the temperature during peak summer (when it's 45°C in Karachi), your products will spoil. Always calculate the cooling load with a 10-15% safety factor, and recommend a Duty/Standby (N+1) setup for critical storage.

4. Poor Door Sealing

The doors are the weakest point of any cold room. If the door frames are not perfectly sealed, or if the door heaters (for freezer rooms) are not working, you will get ice build-up and hot air infiltration. Always use heavy-duty, industrial-grade cold storage doors, and ensure the door sweeps are tight.

5. Forgetting Airflow & Defrost

If the evaporator coils are placed too close to the ceiling or blocked by stored goods, the cold air cannot circulate. This creates hot spots in the room. Furthermore, all freezer rooms must have a proper defrost cycle (electric or hot gas) to prevent the coils from icing over and choking the airflow.

Final Thoughts

A cold room is a system, not just a box with an AC unit. It requires proper thermal engineering, precise installation, and high-quality materials. If you cut corners now, you will pay for it in repairs and lost product later.
