PIR vs PU Sandwich Panels: Which Insulation Core Should You Choose?

When building a cold room or insulated structure, the panel core material is one of the most important decisions. Two options dominate the market: PIR (Polyisocyanurate) and PU/PUF (Polyurethane). Both are excellent insulators, but they differ in fire performance, cost, and specific applications. This guide will help you choose the right one for your project.

1. What Are PU and PIR Panels?

Both PU and PIR are rigid foam plastics formed by reacting isocyanates with polyols. The main difference is the ratio of isocyanate and the chemical structure created during reaction.

  • PU (Polyurethane) — The classic foam, widely used since the 1960s. It has excellent thermal insulation (λ ≈ 0.022 W/m·K) and good adhesion to steel skins.
  • PIR (Polyisocyanurate) — A modified polyurethane with a higher isocyanate index. It has slightly better thermal performance (λ ≈ 0.020 W/m·K) and significantly improved fire resistance.

2. Fire Performance: The Biggest Difference

PropertyPUPIR
Fire class (EN 13501-1)Typically E or FB-s1, d0 or better
Smoke emissionHigherLower
Char formationSoftens and burnsForms a protective char layer
Use in high-risk buildingsLimitedRecommended

For cold storage projects in the Middle East, Europe, or any facility requiring FM Approval or compliance with ASTM E84 / EN 13501-1, PIR is often mandatory. In Pakistan, most local projects use PU due to cost, but for export-oriented or pharma-grade facilities, PIR is strongly recommended.

3. Thermal Insulation

PIR has a slightly lower thermal conductivity, meaning better insulation per millimetre. However, the difference is small (about 10%). In practice, a 100mm PIR panel provides roughly the same insulation as a 110–120mm PU panel.

For most applications, the choice between PU and PIR is driven by fire safety, not just thermal performance.

4. Cost Comparison

  • PU panels are 15–20% cheaper than PIR panels of the same thickness.
  • For a 1,000 ft² cold room, choosing PU over PIR can save Rs. 150,000–300,000 in Pakistan.

But consider the full lifecycle: PIR's better fire resistance can lower insurance premiums and meet stricter building codes, potentially saving money long-term.

5. When to Use PU vs PIR

Choose PU when:

  • The building is a standard chiller or freezer (0°C to -25°C).
  • Local codes accept PU.
  • Budget is a primary concern.
  • The facility is not used for high-value or fire-sensitive goods.

Choose PIR when:

  • The cold room stores pharmaceuticals, high-value food, or chemicals.
  • You need FM Approval or compliance with international fire standards.
  • The building is in a high-fire-risk area (e.g., industrial zones with hazardous materials).
  • You are exporting the project to GCC or European markets.

6. Other Core Options

  • EPS (Expanded Polystyrene) — Cheapest, but lowest insulation and poor fire resistance. Only for above-0°C applications and budget projects.
  • Rockwool — Excellent fire rating but heavier and more expensive. Used in high-hazard industrial buildings.

FAQ

Q: Can I use PU panels for a freezer?

A: Yes, 125–150mm PU is standard for freezers down to -25°C. Below that, consider PIR for better stability.

Q: Is PIR worth the extra cost in Pakistan?

A: For most local commercial projects, PU is sufficient. But if you are exporting, storing pharma products, or need fire certification, PIR is worth it.
