Percentage Formulas Everyone Should Know (But Most People Forget)

Published by ColdMatrix • 5 min read

Percentages show up everywhere — discounts, salary raises, exam scores, tax — yet most people default to guessing instead of calculating. Here are the formulas that cover almost every real situation.

Finding a Percentage of a Number

Formula: (Percentage ÷ 100) × Number. Example: 15% of 2,400 = 0.15 × 2,400 = 360.

Percentage Increase or Decrease

Formula: [(New Value − Original Value) ÷ Original Value] × 100. If your rent went from Rs. 20,000 to Rs. 23,000, the increase is [(23,000−20,000)÷20,000]×100 = 15%.

Reverse Percentage (Finding the Original Value)

If a discounted price is given and you need the original: Original = Discounted Price ÷ (1 − Discount%/100). A Rs. 850 item after a 15% discount had an original price of 850 ÷ 0.85 = Rs. 1,000.

Percentage Points vs Percentage

This trips up even experienced people. If interest rates go from 5% to 7%, that's a 2 percentage point increase, but a 40% relative increase [(7−5)/5×100]. Financial news often blurs this distinction, so always check which one is being reported.

Common Real-World Uses

  • Calculating sales tax or VAT added to a price
  • Working out a tip amount at a restaurant
  • Comparing exam or test scores across different total marks
  • Understanding salary increment percentages
