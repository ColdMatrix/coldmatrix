Percentage Formulas Everyone Should Know (But Most People Forget)

Published by ColdMatrix • 8 min read

Percentages show up everywhere — discounts, salary raises, exam scores, taxes, profit margins — yet most people default to guessing instead of calculating. Understanding a few core formulas can save you money and help you make better decisions in daily life.

Finding a Percentage of a Number

This is the most common calculation. You need to know how much a certain percentage of a given value is.

Formula: (Percentage ÷ 100) × Number

Example: 15% of 2,400 = 0.15 × 2,400 = 360

This is useful for calculating discounts, tips, and VAT amounts.

Percentage Increase

Used to compare growth — salary hike, price increase, population growth, etc.

Formula: [(New Value − Original Value) ÷ Original Value] × 100

Example: If your rent went from Rs. 20,000 to Rs. 23,000, the increase is:
[(23,000 − 20,000) ÷ 20,000] × 100 = 15%

Percentage Decrease

Used when prices drop, discounts are applied, or usage decreases.

Formula: [(Original Value − New Value) ÷ Original Value] × 100

Example: A product price falls from Rs. 800 to Rs. 600. The decrease is:
[(800 − 600) ÷ 800] × 100 = 25%

Reverse Percentage (Finding the Original Value)

If a discounted price is given and you need the original price, use this:

Formula: Original = Discounted Price ÷ (1 − Discount%/100)

Example: A Rs. 850 item after a 15% discount had an original price of:
850 ÷ 0.85 = Rs. 1,000

Percentage Points vs Percentage

This trips up even experienced professionals. If interest rates go from 5% to 7%, that's a 2 percentage point increase, but a 40% relative increase [(7−5)/5×100]. Financial news often blurs this distinction, so always check which one is being reported.

Common Real-World Uses

  • Calculating sales tax or VAT added to a price
  • Working out a tip amount at a restaurant
  • Comparing exam or test scores across different total marks
  • Understanding salary increment percentages
  • Calculating profit margin and markup for business
  • Finding discount amount during sales or promotions

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How do I calculate a discount price quickly?
A: Multiply the original price by (1 − Discount%/100). For example, 20% off Rs. 5,000 = 5,000 × 0.80 = Rs. 4,000.

Q: What is the difference between percentage and percentage points?
A: Percentage is a relative measure. Percentage points are the simple arithmetic difference between two percentages.

Q: Can percentage be more than 100?
A: Yes. If a value more than doubles, the percentage increase will be above 100%.
