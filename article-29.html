What Is a Sandwich Panel?

A sandwich panel is a prefabricated building material made of two outer steel sheets and a rigid insulating core, typically PUF (Polyurethane Foam), PIR (Polyisocyanurate), or EPS (Expanded Polystyrene). These panels are widely used for cold storage rooms, PEB structures, clean rooms, and industrial buildings because they provide excellent insulation, strength, and fast installation in one product.

Standard Sheet & Panel Widths

Globally, steel coils used for sandwich panels come in different widths. The most common full-size PUF/PIR sheet is 1220 mm (4 feet) wide. After profiling, the sheet shrinks slightly — usually by 20 to 30 mm — resulting in a final effective width of approximately 1190 mm.

Other widths include 1200 mm, 1180 mm, and 1150 mm. Always use the correct effective width when calculating panel quantities.

PUF vs PIR — Which Is Better?

PIR is better than PUF in almost every technical aspect — better fire resistance, slightly better insulation, and better dimensional stability. However, PUF is more commonly used because it's more cost-effective. For critical applications where fire safety is a concern, PIR is the recommended choice.

Can Sandwich Panels Be Used for the Floor?

Yes, absolutely. In negative temperature rooms, floor insulation is critical. Typically, floors use XPS or PUF/PIR board sheets. But in many cases, sandwich panels are laid directly on the floor with a finishing sheet on top.

Outer Skin Materials & Thickness

Common outer skins include PPGI, stainless steel, aluminium, and BlueScope steel. Skin thickness ranges from 0.4 mm to 0.7 mm. The coating thickness (microns) matters — aim for at least 20-30 microns for long-term durability.

Cold Storage Doors — Key Considerations

Door hardware must be top quality. Always install PVC curtains in front of doors. For negative temperature rooms, door heaters are essential to prevent ice formation.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Forgetting wastage — always add 3-5% for cuts.
  • Using wrong effective width — many use 1200 mm instead of 1190 mm.
  • Ignoring panel orientation.
  • Not considering core material for the application.
