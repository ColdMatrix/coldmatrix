How to Write a Quotation That Actually Wins the Client

Published by ColdMatrix • 6 min read

A quotation isn't just a price list — it's often the deciding factor between winning a project and losing it to a competitor with a clearer, more professional document. Here's what actually matters.

Structure That Builds Trust

A strong quotation clearly separates: your business details and branding, the client's details, a clear scope of work, itemized pricing, validity period, and payment terms. Missing any of these creates ambiguity that can stall a decision or invite renegotiation later.

Itemize, Don't Lump Sum

A single total figure with no breakdown reads as less trustworthy than a line-item breakdown, even if the total is identical. Clients want to see what they're paying for — materials vs labor, per-unit costs, and any optional add-ons clearly separated.

Always Include a Validity Date

Material and labor costs change. A quotation without an expiry date can come back to bite you months later when a client tries to hold you to outdated pricing — always state "Valid for 15/30 days" or similar.

Be Explicit About What's NOT Included

Disputes most often arise from assumed inclusions. If site preparation, permits, or transport aren't included in your price, say so directly — this single habit prevents the majority of pricing disagreements down the line.

Payment Terms Should Be Unambiguous

  • State deposit percentage and when it's due (e.g., "50% advance, 50% on completion")
  • Specify accepted payment methods
  • Note late payment terms if applicable

Presentation Matters More Than People Think

A branded, well-formatted quotation with your logo and consistent styling signals professionalism before the client reads a single price — and directly affects whether they trust the numbers inside it.
