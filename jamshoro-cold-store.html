Case Study: Engineering a 0°C to 4°C Egg Storage Cold Room in Jamshoro

By Waseem Akhtar
Founder & CEO, Cold Matrix Advanced Solutions
Published: November 2026

When Medina Ice Factory approached us in Jamshoro, they had a specific problem: their layer poultry farm was producing more eggs than they could deliver daily. To prevent spoilage and maintain a steady supply chain, they needed a reliable, energy-efficient cold storage solution on-site.

We recently commissioned their first cold store, and it is now operating flawlessly. Here is a breakdown of the engineering specifications and the decisions we made to optimize this facility.

Project Specifications

  • Location: Jamshoro, Sindh
  • Application: Poultry Layer Egg Storage
  • Room Dimensions: 20 ft × 40 ft × 15 ft (Height)
  • Operating Temperature: 0°C to 4°C
  • Panel Specification: 75 mm PU/PIR Sandwich Panels

Engineering Decisions: Why 75mm Panels?

For a room this size, one might initially assume a thicker panel is required. However, this cold room was built indoors inside a concrete hall. Because the facility is protected from direct solar heat gain and extreme ambient temperatures (which Jamshoro is famous for), the thermal load on the envelope was significantly reduced.

We specified 75 mm PU/PIR sandwich panels, which provided the perfect balance of high thermal efficiency and cost-effectiveness for an indoor application.

Refrigeration & Redundancy

To maintain the strict 0°C to 4°C range required for safe egg storage, we installed two separate 7.5 HP condensing units. This dual-system setup is configured for Duty/Standby (N+1 Redundancy):

  • Under Partial Load: Just one unit runs to maintain the temperature efficiently.
  • Under Full Load: Both units run simultaneously to handle the high thermal load.
  • Backup Protection: If one unit fails, the second automatically kicks in. The eggs are never left unprotected.

Floor Execution: The Hidden Details

Even though the cold store was built inside, the ground beneath the concrete holds a warmer temperature. Without a thermal break, the concrete would freeze and crack. Here is the exact sequence we used for the floor:

  1. XPS Insulation: We laid 100mm XPS (Extruded Polystyrene) sheets evenly and fixed them securely to prevent movement.
  2. The Vapor Barrier: We covered the XPS with a high-quality, heavy-duty plastic sheet. This prevents moisture from the wet concrete from seeping into the insulation.
  3. Concrete Screed: The client poured the concrete flooring over the plastic barrier, providing a solid, load-bearing surface.

Final Handover & The Business Impact

With the floor cured, we installed the 75mm PU/PIR panels for the walls and ceiling, followed by heavy-duty insulated cold room doors to ensure an airtight seal. Once the system was connected and commissioned, we monitored the pull-down temperature to ensure it reliably held the 0°C to 4°C range.

The cold store is now running perfectly. Medina Ice Factory can safely store their surplus eggs. The success of this project has already resulted in a contract for five additional cold stores in the queue.
