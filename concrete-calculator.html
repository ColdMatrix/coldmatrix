What Is Concrete?

Concrete is a composite material made from cement, sand, aggregate, and water. It is the most widely used construction material in the world, with over 10 billion tons produced annually globally. Its strength, durability, and versatility make it essential for foundations, slabs, columns, beams, roads, and bridges.

Concrete Mix Ratios Explained

The mix ratio represents the proportion of cement, sand, and aggregate. Common ratios include:

  • 1:2:4 — General purpose (slabs, footings). Approximate strength: 15–20 MPa.
  • 1:1.5:3 — Structural elements (columns, beams). Approximate strength: 20–25 MPa.
  • 1:3:6 — Lean concrete (blinding, leveling). Lower strength.

How the Calculator Works

The calculator uses the dry volume method, which accounts for the fact that dry materials shrink when mixed with water. It multiplies the wet volume by 1.54 to estimate the dry volume, then distributes it according to the mix ratio. Material densities used are: cement 1440 kg/m³, sand 1600 kg/m³, aggregate 1500 kg/m³.

Real-World Example

For a slab 10 m × 8 m × 0.15 m thick using a 1:2:4 mix:

  • Wet volume = 10 × 8 × 0.15 = 12 m³
  • Dry volume = 12 × 1.54 = 18.48 m³
  • Cement = (1/7) × 18.48 × 1440 ≈ 3,802 kg ≈ 76 bags of 50 kg
  • Sand = (2/7) × 18.48 × 1600 ≈ 8,448 kg ≈ 8.45 tons
  • Aggregate = (4/7) × 18.48 × 1500 ≈ 15,840 kg ≈ 15.84 tons

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Forgetting the dry volume factor (1.54) — leads to underestimation.
  • Using the wrong mix ratio for the structural element.
  • Ignoring water quantity — too much water weakens concrete.
  • Not accounting for wastage — always add 5–10% extra.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: What mix ratio should I use for a house slab?
A: 1:2:4 is standard for general house slabs. For structural elements like columns, use 1:1.5:3.

Q: Why multiply by 1.54?
A: Dry materials occupy more volume. Adding water compacts them. The 1.54 factor accounts for this bulkage.

Q: Can I use this for footings?
A: Yes, just enter the footing dimensions as length, width, and thickness.
