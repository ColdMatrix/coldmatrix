PUF vs PIR Sandwich Panels: Which One Should You Choose?

Published by ColdMatrix • 7 min read

If you're planning a cold room, freezer, or PEB structure, the panel material you choose affects your insulation performance, fire safety, budget, and how long the building lasts. The two most common options are PUF (Polyurethane Foam) and PIR (Polyisocyanurate). They look almost identical from the outside, but the core chemistry is different — and that difference matters more than most quotations let on.

What's the Core Difference?

PUF and PIR both start from similar polyol and isocyanate chemistry, but PIR is manufactured with a modified ratio and a trimerization process that makes the foam more thermally stable and significantly more fire-resistant. PUF is cheaper to produce and has slightly better raw insulation value per millimeter, but it burns more easily and degrades faster under sustained heat.

Side-by-Side Comparison

PropertyPUFPIR
Thermal conductivity~0.022–0.024 W/mK~0.023–0.026 W/mK
Fire ratingCombustible, low fire resistanceFire-retardant, self-extinguishing, better Class B/C ratings
Cost10–20% cheaperHigher upfront cost
Best use caseStandard cold rooms, budget projects, interior partitionsFreezer rooms, food factories, projects needing fire-code compliance
Typical lifespan10–15 years15–25 years

When to Choose PUF

  • Budget-sensitive projects where fire code requirements are minimal
  • Interior office partitions or non-food storage cold rooms
  • Short-to-medium term structures

When to Choose PIR

  • Food processing facilities, pharma cold storage, or any space with strict fire-safety inspections
  • Freezer rooms below -20°C where long-term thermal stability matters
  • Projects where insurance or building codes require fire-retardant materials

A Practical Rule of Thumb

If your client's insurance provider or local building authority asks about fire rating, go with PIR — the price difference is usually recovered within the first few years through lower insurance premiums and fewer compliance issues. For everyday cold storage without strict fire-code scrutiny, PUF remains a solid, cost-effective choice.
