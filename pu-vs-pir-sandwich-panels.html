PU vs. PIR Sandwich Panels: A Case Study at IM Academy

By Waseem Akhtar
Founder & CEO, Cold Matrix Advanced Solutions
Published: November 2026

As an engineer or contractor, one of the most common questions you will face when specifying insulated panels is: "Should we use PU or PIR?" They look identical, they both offer excellent thermal insulation, and they are both made of metal facings bonded to an insulated core. But the difference between them can mean the difference between passing a fire safety inspection—or failing it.

Here is the practical, real-world breakdown of PU vs. PIR, based on our recent project for a prominent educational institution.

The Core Difference: Fire Performance

Both PU (Polyurethane) and PIR (Polyisocyanurate) are excellent insulators. The primary difference lies in their chemical structure and how they react to extreme heat and fire.

  • PU (Polyurethane): While it offers great insulation, PU tends to melt and drip when exposed to high temperatures. It can also contribute to the spread of flames.
  • PIR (Polyisocyanurate): PIR is modified during manufacturing to improve its fire resistance. When exposed to fire, PIR forms a protective outer layer of "char" rather than melting away. This char layer prevents the fire from penetrating the panel and slows down the spread of flames significantly.

The Bottom Line: If the project involves public safety, high occupancy, or strict building codes, PIR is the mandatory choice.

Real-World Case Study: IM Academy, Karachi

Recently, we were contracted by IM Academy, an early childhood and Montessori school in Scheme 33, Karachi. They needed to build a comprehensive hall for extracurricular activities, where young students would gather for presentations and projector sessions.

Because the hall was designed for young children (Play Group to Primary levels), safety and temperature control were the highest priorities. Here is how we solved their problem:

  • The Old Roof: They were previously using standard fiberglass sheets, which offered almost zero protection against Karachi's brutal summer heat and were not fire-safe.
  • The New Build: We constructed a 14 ft × 18 ft hall with an 8 ft height, perfectly sized for their Montessori activities.
  • The Material: We recommended and installed PU/PIR Sandwich Panels for the roof. PIR was chosen primarily for fire safety—because when you have children inside a hall, you do not compromise on safety.
  • The Finish: We installed UPVC doors and windows with glass to complete the enclosure, ensuring a bright and secure environment.

The Result: The PIR core gave the school the fire resistance required for a high-occupancy children's environment. At the same time, the thermal mass of the sandwich panels now completely protects the students from Karachi's extreme summer heat and winter cold, while the old fiberglass previously did neither.

My Rule of Thumb for Clients

When a client wants to save money and asks to use PU instead of PIR, I ask them one question: "What is inside this building?"

If it is a standard cold room storing non-combustible goods, PU might be a cost-effective option. But if it is a food processing plant, a residential structure, or a school hall like IM Academy with people inside, always specify PIR. The difference in cost is minor compared to the difference in safety.
