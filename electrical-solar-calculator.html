📖 The Complete Solar System Design Guide

This comprehensive calculator helps you plan every aspect of your electrical and solar energy system — from calculating your daily electrical load to sizing solar panels, batteries, inverters, and cables. Whether you're designing a home backup system, an off-grid cabin, or a commercial solar installation, this guide and calculator give you the knowledge and tools you need.

What Is an Electrical Load?

Electrical load is the total amount of power consumed by all your electrical appliances and devices, measured in watts (W) or kilowatts (kW). Understanding your load is the foundation of any solar system design because it determines how much energy you need to generate, store, and distribute.

Connected Load is the sum of all appliance wattages if everything runs at once. Actual Consumption is what you really use based on hours of operation. The difference is significant — most homes only use 30–50% of their connected load at any time.

Step 1: Calculating Daily Energy Consumption

Daily energy consumption = Sum of (Quantity × Wattage × Hours per day) for all appliances.

Example:

  • 5 LED lights × 15W × 6 hours = 450Wh
  • 3 fans × 75W × 8 hours = 1,800Wh
  • 1 refrigerator × 200W × 24 hours = 4,800Wh
  • 1 TV × 100W × 4 hours = 400Wh

Total = 7,450Wh = 7.45 kWh per day

Step 2: Solar Panel Sizing

Solar panels convert sunlight into DC electricity. To size your array:

Required Solar Capacity (kW) = Daily Consumption (kWh) ÷ (Peak Sun Hours × System Efficiency)

Peak sun hours vary by location — typically 4–6 hours in Pakistan, India, and the Middle East. System efficiency (75–85%) accounts for losses from wiring, inverters, temperature, and panel degradation.

Example: 7.45 kWh ÷ (5 × 0.8) = 1.86 kW. With 550W panels: 1860 ÷ 550 = 4 panels.

Step 3: Battery Backup Sizing

Batteries store solar energy for use at night or during outages. Sizing depends on backup hours, voltage, capacity, and depth of discharge (DoD).

Battery Energy (Wh) = (Connected Load × Backup Hours) ÷ (DoD × Efficiency)

  • Lead Acid: 50% DoD — cheaper but shorter life
  • Lithium-ion: 80–90% DoD — higher efficiency, longer life
  • Gel: 50% DoD — maintenance-free

Step 4: Inverter Sizing

The inverter converts DC to AC. It must handle your maximum load plus surge for motors and compressors.

Inverter Size = Maximum Load × (1 + Surge%) × (1 + Safety Margin%)

Example: 3000W × 1.25 × 1.20 = 4,500W ≈ 5 kVA inverter.

Step 5: Cable Sizing

Proper cable sizing prevents overheating, voltage drop, and fire risk. Cable area depends on current, voltage, length, conductor material (copper vs aluminum), and allowable voltage drop (2–3% typical).

Solar Panel Types Comparison

  • Monocrystalline: Highest efficiency (18–22%). Best for limited space.
  • Polycrystalline: Lower cost, slightly lower efficiency (15–17%).
  • Thin-Film: Flexible, lightweight, lower efficiency (10–12%).

Battery Types Comparison

  • Lead Acid: $100–200/kWh, 3–5 year life, 50% DoD
  • Lithium-ion: $300–500/kWh, 10–15 year life, 90% DoD
  • Gel: $150–250/kWh, 5–7 year life, 50% DoD

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Underestimating consumption: Add 20–25% margin for future needs.
  • Using wrong battery type: Match DoD and cycle life to your budget.
  • Ignoring peak sun hours: Generation varies by season and location.
  • Undersized cables: Causes overheating, fire risk, and voltage loss.
  • Not considering surge requirements: Motors and pumps draw extra starting current.
  • Forgetting system losses: Every component has efficiency losses.

Real-World Applications

  • Home Backup: Power essential appliances during outages.
  • Off-Grid Homes: Complete self-sufficient energy systems.
  • Commercial Solar: Reduce electricity bills for businesses.
  • Agricultural: Power water pumps, irrigation, and farm equipment.
  • Telecom Towers: Reliable remote power for communication.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I run my entire house on solar?
A: Yes, if your solar capacity and battery storage match your daily consumption and backup needs.

Q: How many solar panels do I need for a home?
A: An average home using 10 kWh/day needs 8–12 panels of 550W each.

Q: What battery type is best?
A: Lithium-ion — higher efficiency, longer life, and deeper discharge. Lead acid is cheaper but requires more maintenance.

Q: Does this calculator work for three-phase systems?
A: Yes, it supports single-phase and three-phase cable sizing.

Q: Is solar power worth it in Pakistan?
A: Yes, Pakistan receives 5–6 peak sun hours daily, making solar highly viable with payback periods of 3–5 years.

Q: How long do solar panels last?
A: Most panels come with 25-year performance warranties, with 80% output guaranteed after 25 years.

Q: What maintenance does a solar system need?
A: Periodic panel cleaning, checking connections, and monitoring battery health.

Q: Can I expand my solar system later?
A: Yes, if your inverter and charge controller have spare capacity.
