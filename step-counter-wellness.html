Step Counter & Motion Sensor Telemetry Guide

The ColdMatrix Step Counter uses your phone's built-in motion sensor (DeviceMotionEvent) to detect the rhythmic acceleration pattern of walking. Tap "Start Tracking" and keep your phone on you — in a pocket or in hand — while you walk.

Browser & permission notes: Motion sensor access requires a secure HTTPS connection, and some browsers ask you to grant motion permission the first time you tap Start. If your browser or device doesn't expose motion data, step counting will stay at zero rather than fake a number — you can still log your day using the Reset/manual approach.

Heart rate & blood pressure: A phone browser cannot measure your real heart rate or blood pressure without dedicated hardware. Use the Measure/Log buttons to record readings you've taken with a proper device (smartwatch, BP monitor, or manual pulse count) — the app stores and tracks them for you, it doesn't invent them.

Understanding Step Counting Accuracy

Step counters rely on the device's accelerometer to detect the bounce of your body as you walk. The accuracy depends on several factors:

  • Carry position: Pocket carry or natural arm swing gives the best results. Holding the phone still while looking at the screen produces less motion for the sensor to detect.
  • Device sensor quality: Different phones have different motion sensors. Newer devices typically have more sensitive and accurate sensors.
  • Walking speed and pattern: Normal walking produces clear acceleration peaks. Very slow shuffling or unusual gait may be harder to detect.
  • Environmental factors: Bumpy roads or vibrating surfaces may cause false readings, though the algorithm includes filtering to reduce this.

Practical tip: For the most consistent tracking, place your phone in a front pocket or use an armband. Avoid holding it in a bag that doesn't move with your body.

Motion Sensor Permissions & Browser Support

Modern browsers require user permission before accessing motion sensors. On iOS, you'll see a prompt when you first tap "Start Tracking." On Android, permission is usually granted automatically, but you can check site settings if tracking doesn't start.

If your browser or device doesn't support the DeviceMotionEvent API, the step counter cannot automatically count steps. In that case, you can:

  • Use a dedicated pedometer app and manually enter your steps.
  • Use a fitness band or smartwatch and log your activity.
  • Update your browser to the latest version.

Health Advisories: Heart Rate

A normal resting heart rate for adults ranges from 60 to 100 beats per minute (BPM). Lower rates are common in athletes and people who exercise regularly. You should be aware of the following:

  • High resting heart rate (>100 BPM): May indicate stress, dehydration, illness, or underlying heart conditions. Consult a doctor if persistent.
  • Low resting heart rate (<60 BPM): Can be normal for fit individuals, but may cause dizziness or fatigue in others.
  • Post-exercise heart rate: Usually elevated; should return to near resting within several minutes.

Important: This app does not measure heart rate directly. It only stores and displays values you manually enter. Never rely on a phone camera or manual pulse count for medical decisions — use a proper heart rate monitor.

Health Advisories: Blood Pressure

Blood pressure is recorded as two numbers: systolic (pressure when the heart beats) over diastolic (pressure when the heart rests). Normal is generally below 120/80 mmHg.

  • Elevated: 120–129 / less than 80
  • High Blood Pressure (Stage 1): 130–139 / 80–89
  • High Blood Pressure (Stage 2): 140 or higher / 90 or higher
  • Hypertensive Crisis: Higher than 180 / higher than 120 — seek medical attention immediately

Always use a clinically validated blood pressure monitor. Log your readings regularly and share trends with your healthcare provider. A single reading does not diagnose hypertension.

Hydration Guidelines

Water is essential for nearly every bodily function. General daily intake recommendations vary, but a common guideline is around 2.5 to 3.0 liters for men and 2.0 to 2.5 liters for women, including all fluids.

  • Hot weather or high activity: Increase intake to replace sweat losses.
  • Signs of dehydration: Thirst, dark urine, fatigue, dizziness, and dry mouth.
  • Overhydration: Rare but possible; avoid drinking excessive amounts in a short time.

Log your water intake throughout the day to stay on track. This app allows quick addition of 250 ml or 500 ml increments.

Exercise & Calorie Estimation

The Exercise card shows estimated distance, calories, and active minutes based on your step count. These are rough estimates using standard formulas:

  • Distance: Estimated stride length × number of steps.
  • Calories: Approximately 0.04 kcal per step for a person of average weight.
  • Active minutes: Based on the assumption that 100–120 steps equal one minute of moderate activity.

For more accurate calorie burn, use a fitness tracker that accounts for your weight, height, age, and heart rate.

Streak System & Goal Setting

Your daily step goal is your target for the day. The app tracks whether you reach that goal and builds a consecutive-day streak.

  • Set a realistic goal: Start with a number you can consistently achieve, then gradually increase.
  • Miss a day? Your streak resets, but your previous days' data remains saved.
  • Celebration: Reaching your goal triggers a special animation and updates your streak.

Consistency matters more than occasional big numbers. Review your weekly report to understand your patterns and adjust your goal accordingly.

Privacy & Data Storage

All data is stored locally on your device using localStorage. Nothing is uploaded to any server. This means:

  • Your health data stays private and only you can access it.
  • If you clear your browser data, the app will reset to defaults.
  • There is no account or cloud sync; each device maintains its own data.

For persistent storage across sessions, avoid using incognito/private mode, which clears data when the tab closes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Why is my step count not increasing even after I tap Start?
A: Make sure you have granted motion sensor permission. Also, ensure you are actually moving — the sensor needs movement to detect steps.

Q: Can I use this on more than one device?
A: Yes, but data does not sync between devices. Each device stores its own history.

Q: Is the heart rate feature accurate?
A: The app does not measure heart rate. You must enter readings manually from a separate device.

Q: Does the step counter work offline?
A: Yes, once the page is loaded, all tracking and storage happen locally.

Q: What happens at midnight?
A: The app automatically rolls over to a new day. Yesterday's steps are saved in history, and today's counter resets.

Q: How do I change my daily goal?
A: Tap the "GOAL" button on the main card or the Settings icon in the bottom navigation.
