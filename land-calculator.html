🌍 Complete Guide to Global Land Measurement Units

Land measurement is one of the oldest and most culturally diverse systems in human history. Unlike distance or weight, which have been standardized globally under the metric system, land area is still measured using deeply local units that vary by country, region, and even district. This is why a farmer in Punjab speaks of "Marla" while a buyer in Texas speaks of "Acres," and a real estate agent in Tokyo talks about "Tsubo."

Our ColdMatrix Land Area Calculator solves this global problem. Enter a value in any unit — and instantly see it converted to 30+ units used across Asia, the Middle East, Europe, North America, and the Far East.

Why Land Units Differ Around the World

Land units were historically based on practical, human-scale measurements — how much land a pair of oxen could plough in a day, how much a family needed to survive, or how much seed could be sown on a given plot. Because different civilizations developed independently, they created their own scales. Even today, these traditional units remain in active use because property records, tax systems, and inheritance laws are often built on them.

South Asian Land Units Explained

South Asia has the richest variety of land units in the world. Here's what each one means:

  • Marla: The smallest common unit in Pakistan and North India. Traditionally 1 Marla = 272.25 square feet (approximately 25.29 m²). Some modern housing societies use 225 sq ft Marla. 20 Marla = 1 Kanal.
  • Kanal: Equal to 20 Marla or 5,445 square feet (approximately 505.86 m²). Commonly used for large residential plots and small farms.
  • Murabba: Equal to 25 Acres. Used for very large agricultural estates. 1 Murabba = 25 Acres = 500 Kanal.
  • Acre: The standard British-era unit, still used across Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, and the West. 1 Acre = 4,046.86 m² = 43,560 sq ft.
  • Bigha: A traditional agricultural unit with different values across regions. In Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, 1 Bigha = 2,529 m². In West Bengal, it's smaller (~1,338 m²). In Rajasthan and Gujarat, it's around 1,619 m².
  • Katha: Used in Bihar, UP, and Bengal. In Bihar/UP, 1 Katha = 126.44 m². In Bengal, it's 66.89 m².
  • Chatak: A small Bengali unit. 16 Chatak = 1 Katha (Bengal).
  • Cent: Used in South India (Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka). 1 Cent = 1/100 Acre = 40.47 m².
  • Ground: Common in Tamil Nadu. 1 Ground = 2,400 sq ft = 222.97 m².
  • Guntha: Used in Maharashtra. 1 Guntha = 1/40 Acre = 101.17 m².

Imperial / US Units

The imperial system remains dominant in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and former British colonies:

  • Square Foot (ft²): The most common US residential unit. 1 ft² ≈ 0.0929 m².
  • Square Yard (yd²): Used in UK, India, Pakistan for carpets and small plots. 1 yd² = 9 ft² = 0.836 m².
  • Acre: 43,560 sq ft = 4,046.86 m². Standard US land unit.
  • Square Mile: 640 Acres = 2.59 km². Used for large territories.
  • Rood: An old British unit equal to 1/4 Acre = 1,011.71 m².

Middle East Units

  • Dunam: Used in Israel, Jordan, Lebanon, and Palestine. 1 Dunam = 1,000 m² (the "metric dunam"). Older Ottoman dunams were 919.3 m².
  • Feddan: Egypt's primary agricultural unit. 1 Feddan = 4,200.83 m² (roughly 1.038 Acres).
  • Kilometric Dunam: 10,000 m², used in some Gulf countries for large plots.

Far East Units

  • Tsubo (Japan): Standard Japanese real estate unit. 1 Tsubo = 3.30579 m² = 2 tatami mats.
  • Pyeong (Korea): Korean equivalent. 1 Pyeong = 3.30579 m².
  • Tan (Japan): 300 Tsubo = 991.74 m².
  • Cho (Japan): 10 Tan = 9,917.36 m² ≈ 1 Hectare.
  • Mu (China): Standard Chinese agricultural unit. 1 Mu = 666.67 m² (15 Mu = 1 Hectare).
  • Rai (Thailand): 1 Rai = 1,600 m² = 4 Ngan = 400 Wah.

Metric System

The internationally recognized standard used by scientists, governments, and most of the world:

  • Square Meter (m²): Base unit of area in the metric system.
  • Are: 100 m². Rarely used directly but is the base for hectare.
  • Hectare (ha): 10,000 m² = 2.471 Acres. Standard for large land.
  • Square Kilometer (km²): 1,000,000 m². Used for cities and countries.

Best Practices for Land Transactions

  • Always confirm the local standard: A "Marla" can be 272.25 or 225 sq ft depending on region and developer. Ask your lawyer or society office which standard applies.
  • Convert to square feet or square meters: When comparing plots from different regions, always convert to a common unit.
  • Check the legal document: Your property title will list the unit officially used in your area's land registry.
  • Use multiple units: Cross-check by entering the value in 2–3 different units to confirm consistency.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How many square feet in 1 Marla?
A: Traditional Marla = 272.25 sq ft. Some modern societies use 225 sq ft. This tool has both.

Q: How many Kanal in 1 Acre?

Q: Is this calculator accurate for legal use?
A: The math is precise. However, always verify with your local land registry or a certified surveyor for legal transactions, as some regions have local variations.

A: Bigha varies by state — UP/Bihar (~2,529 m²), West Bengal (~1,338 m²), Rajasthan/Gujarat (~1,619 m²). Choose the correct variant for your state.
