CFT Explained — Cubic Feet Calculations for Timber, Stone, and Sand

Published by ColdMatrix • 5 min read

If you've ever bought timber, stone, or sand for a construction project, you've likely encountered CFT — a unit that confuses newcomers but remains the trading standard across the region.

What CFT Means

CFT stands for Cubic Feet — a volume measurement equal to a cube one foot on each side (1 ft × 1 ft × 1 ft). It's used to measure and price bulk materials like timber logs, dressed wood, stone blocks, and loose sand or aggregate.

The Basic Formula

CFT = Length (ft) × Width (ft) × Height/Thickness (ft). For a wooden beam 10 ft long, 1 ft wide, and 0.5 ft thick: CFT = 10 × 1 × 0.5 = 5 CFT.

Why CFT Persists in an Increasingly Metric World

Timber and stone trading in South Asia has used CFT as the standard pricing unit for generations — suppliers, contractors, and pricing sheets are all built around it, making a full switch to cubic meters impractical despite most other construction measurements moving toward metric.

Converting CFT to Cubic Meters

1 CFT = 0.0283 cubic meters. So 100 CFT of timber equals approximately 2.83 cubic meters — useful when comparing prices against international or metric-based suppliers.

A Practical Tip When Buying

Always confirm whether a quoted price is per CFT or for the total quantity — this is a common source of confusion, especially with larger orders, and can lead to significant pricing misunderstandings if assumed incorrectly.
